Real Estate Fraud & Land-Record Risk Atlas of India

Built from RERA/Ministry of Housing enforcement data, NCRB's Crime in India report, the Benami Transactions Act attachment record, and the Department of Land Resources' DILRMP dashboard — no invented state scores.

Why there's no single "risk score out of 10" per state here. No government body publishes a unified real-estate-fraud index by state — RERA, NCRB, the Income Tax Department and the Land Resources Ministry each track a different slice of the problem, on different timelines, using different units. Rather than blend them into one invented number, this atlas keeps each dataset separate and labeled, so every figure traces back to a specific, checkable source (Section 6).

RERA — consumer complaints & enforcement, by state

Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs data via ANAROCK, as of 28 Nov 2023

Nationally, State RERA authorities had resolved 1,16,300 homebuyer complaints and registered 1,16,117 projects and 82,755 agents. Three states account for 69% of all resolved complaints — a strong proxy for where builder/project disputes are most concentrated, though it also partly reflects which states have the most active real estate markets and the most functional RERA benches.

Land-record digitisation — the structural fix for title fraud

05

Common real estate fraud patterns in India

Drawn from RERA orders, ED typologies & media/court reporting — generic, not scored per state

These are the patterns that recur across Indian real estate markets, per RERA adjudication orders and enforcement reporting. As with Section 4, no credible public dataset scores each of these per state, so they're presented as a reference list rather than a colored grid.

01
Revenue record manipulation
Tampering with mutation entries (patta / khata / 7-12 extract / jamabandi) to alter who the land records show as owner.
02
Forged sale deeds & power of attorney
Fabricated or backdated deeds and GPA documents used to claim or transfer ownership without the real owner's consent.
03
Multiple sale of the same unit
A single flat or plot sold to more than one buyer — a recurring complaint category in RERA filings against under-construction projects.
04
Government / gram panchayat land encroachment
Private sale or development of land that actually belongs to the state, forest, or a local body.
05
Illegal layouts & unauthorised colonies
Plots sold in layouts that were never approved by the local development authority, common on urban peripheries.
06
Benami / nominee ownership
Property held in someone else's name to conceal the real, beneficial owner — the specific target of the Benami Act above.
07
Builder fund diversion
Buyer deposits or project-linked loans redirected to other projects or personal use, a central issue in RERA's escrow-account rule.
08
Agricultural land conversion fraud
Selling agricultural land for residential/commercial use before the required land-use conversion has actually been approved.
09
Fake NOCs & approvals
Forged no-objection certificates, occupancy certificates, or environmental/CRZ clearances presented to buyers as genuine.
10
Title disputes from unclear inheritance
Sales proceeding despite unresolved succession claims among legal heirs, common with older, undivided family land.
06

Buyer safeguards

General due-diligence practice, not state-specific data
✓Verify the title chain as far back as records allow, ideally 30 years.
✓Cross-check revenue records (mutation entries) against a physical survey.
✓Confirm the land isn't government, forest, or under litigation.
✓Check the project's RERA registration number on the state RERA portal directly.
✓Validate layout/building approvals with the local development authority.
✓Confirm any power of attorney is current, registered, and not revoked.
✓Ask for the encumbrance certificate covering the full ownership period.
✓Use independent legal vetting, not just the lender's or builder's paperwork.
